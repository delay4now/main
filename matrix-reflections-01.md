# Reflections on the Movie *The Matrix*

I have been asked to write this review for a while. It may not be written too well, but now is better than never, and I believe the 
aim of this effort is participation and stimulating discussions.  

I saw [The Matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Matrix) for the first time over 10 years ago. The movie starts with Neo, a computer hacker that realizes that something is not "right" in his world. He seeks to explain it and his searches lead him to a different group of hackers led by Morpheus. Morpheus asserts that the world is a computer simulation, but says that to know more Neo has to go down the "rabbit hole". Morpheus then makes a proposition that became synonymous with the movie: choose either the blue pill or the red pill, where the blue pill represents going back to old life (and forgetting about it all), and the red pill offers knowing the truth (but nothing more than that). Neo makes his choice, and the movie continues from there.

## This is a classic hero story.

The movie is good, it has several  memorable moments, a nice philosophical touch and a bunch of action.
The two sequels are reasonable (but the third sequel, much more recent, not so much).
I didn't think too much of the movie at the time. 
Later on though, I found myself going back to it often.  
It has many catchy-sounding phrases that you don't think much of at first, until they receive a new meaning in your mind. 

> Why do my eyes hurt? Because you never used them. 

It is painful to live for a long while in some reality, only to one day, abruptly find out it was all fake.

> What the oracle said is for you alone. 

The whole discussion of cause and effect, free will vs determinism. Do we have agency or are we all preprogrammed puppets?

> I know this steak doesn't exist. When I put it in my mouth, the Matrix is telling my brain it is juicy and delicious. After 9 years, you know what I realize? Ignorance is bliss.

The choice of not knowing as a defense mechanism. Though, it is hard to "forget" after knowing certain things. 

The last one I want to mention feels especially relevant with modern AI systems:

> There are endless fields where human beings are no longer born. We are grown. 

In the movie, role of human body is to generate electricity for the machines to consume, like a battery, while the 
mind is kept trapped in the Matrix. 

Are some of these interpretations projections of myself? Sure, but maybe the point is to get you thinking. 

Interestingly, I learnt from a friend that the credits soundtrack ([see here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=re7nmrDbAG4)) is a Sanskrit prayer from the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads) whose rough translation is: 

*Lead me from the unreal to the real.
Lead me from darkness to light.
Lead me from death to immortality.
Om, peace, peace, peace.*
